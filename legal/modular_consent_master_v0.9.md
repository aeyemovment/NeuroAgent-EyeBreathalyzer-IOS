Every signed version must be approved by the responsible IRB/ethics committee and privacy/legal reviewers, contain no unresolved placeholders, and include only the modules that apply.

How this master works

Core participant consent: study participation, procedures, risks, confidentiality, voluntary participation, and signatures.

Granular data permissions: raw media, derived signals, future research, AI/model development, commercial research, record linkage, recontact, and approved external sharing.

Destination-specific permissions: NeuroAgent AI repository, institution-controlled repository, approved country/regional repository, and local/federated processing where supported.

Jurisdiction and site addenda: U.S. federal/HIPAA, state biometric and consumer-health/privacy notices, EU/EEA, UK, Canada, Australia, Brazil, and a deployment hold for unsupported jurisdictions.

Capacity pathway: participant consent when capable; legally authorized representative permission and participant assent when applicable; separate caregiver/study-partner consent.

6. Special notice about biometric-related data

For this form, biometric-related data means recordings or measurements of physical, physiological, or behavioral characteristics, including face and eye images, voice and speech, eye movements, pupils, facial movements, head pose, gait, balance, tapping, typing, and similar signals. Some laws use narrower definitions and regulate data only when it is used or intended to identify a person. This study applies heightened protections to these signals even when they are used for health research rather than identity.

Default rule: The study will not create a faceprint, voiceprint, iris/retina template, gait identity template, or other biometric identifier for authentication, unique identification, surveillance, or tracking unless a separately approved module expressly states that purpose and obtains the required separate permission.

10. What are the risks and discomforts?

Privacy and confidentiality: accidental access, breach, unauthorized sharing, or misuse could reveal health, cognitive, behavioral, voice, face, movement, or identity-related information.

Re-identification: recordings and detailed human signals can remain identifying even after names are removed. Rare-disease combinations can increase this risk.

Emotional or social harm: questions or performance may cause worry, embarrassment, frustration, fatigue, stigma, discrimination concerns, or family tension.

Physical risk: standing, balance, or walking tasks may cause dizziness, fatigue, or a fall. Do not perform a movement task unless the study's safety instructions are satisfied. You may stop immediately.

Incorrect or biased outputs: devices, lighting, language, disability, speech or motor impairment, skin tone, age, and other factors may affect data quality or model performance. ALS-related motor or speech changes may be mistaken for cognitive change if not properly accounted for.

International access: another country's laws may permit government or legal access that differs from protections in your home jurisdiction.

Unknown risks: future analytical methods may reveal information that is not currently predictable.

Study-specific risks: {{STUDY_SPECIFIC_RISKS}}

11. How will the study protect my data?

Collect only data needed for approved purposes; separate direct identifiers from research data where feasible; and use a coded participant ID.

Encrypt data during transmission and storage using the study-approved methods; apply role-based access, least privilege, access logging, and periodic review.

Use written agreements with processors, repositories, and data recipients; restrict onward sharing; and maintain incident and deletion workflows.

Keep backups in disclosed regions, prohibit advertising trackers in research flows, and avoid collecting precise location or unrelated device data unless specifically approved and disclosed.

No security system can eliminate all risk. The study-specific security and privacy controls are {{SECURITY_CONTROL_SUMMARY}}. If a reportable breach occurs, affected participants and authorities will be notified as required by applicable law and the approved response plan.

14. What happens if I stop or withdraw permission?

You may stop study activities, withdraw permission for future collection or optional uses, and request access, correction, restriction, portability, or deletion when those rights apply. Use {{WITHDRAWAL_METHOD}}. The study will confirm the request and explain what action was taken.

Future collection will stop after the request is authenticated and processed, subject to the timeframe required by applicable law.

Identifiable or coded data not yet used may be deleted or returned where the protocol, repository role, and law permit or require.

It may be technically impossible to isolate the statistical influence of one record from a completed model. Requests for deletion or model unlearning will be evaluated under applicable law, the protocol, and the data-governance plan.

When more than one independent repository holds a copy, the responsible controller will route or coordinate the request as required. The response will identify any repository that requires a separate request.

3. Consent dependency and enforcement rules

Do not initialize camera, microphone, motion sensors, local recordings, server upload, analytics, or nonessential logs until required consent and notices are complete.

A destination choice authorizes storage/deposit only. Enforce separate permissions for processing purpose, recipient access, sharing, external research, AI/model development, commercial research, and cross-border transfer.

If raw-media retention is declined but derived measures are allowed, process ephemerally, verify successful derived-data receipt, and delete temporary raw copies and queues according to the disclosed deadline. Record deletion evidence without retaining the media.

If a study requires raw media or a particular repository, label it required before consent. Declining means the person cannot join that study; do not convert the required item into a coerced optional toggle.

When multiple repositories hold copies, maintain copy lineage and recipient identifiers so withdrawal, correction, breach, and deletion notices can cascade. Show unresolved downstream requests to the participant and privacy team.

Do not treat deidentification as permanent or absolute for voice, face, eye, gait, genetic, or rare-disease data. Apply a documented re-identification risk review before release or broader sharing.

No advertising SDKs, session replay, social pixels, or unrelated telemetry may operate on consent, assessment, results, or participant dashboards.

Do not collect precise GPS or infer a legal jurisdiction solely from IP address. Ask residence and current collection location; collect only the minimum technical evidence needed to support the ruleset decision.

A new purpose, data category, recipient class, repository, access country, transfer mechanism, material retention extension, or biometric identification use triggers prospective review and, where required, re-consent before affected processing.

If a participant moves or performs follow-up in a new jurisdiction, re-evaluate the ruleset and apply the stricter applicable control until local review is complete.

Open-source releases must exclude secrets, repository credentials, production endpoints, consent records, data schemas containing real participant examples, and all participant data. Demonstration data must be synthetic or separately approved.

The app must generate a human-readable signed copy and a machine-verifiable consent receipt. The receipt records choices and provenance but is not a substitute for the participant-facing form.

5. Capacity and ALS-FTD safeguards

Use a protocol-defined, decision-specific capacity process; do not infer incapacity from diagnosis, dysarthria, weakness, poor dexterity, eye-gaze access, language difference, or cognitive task score alone.

Offer communication accommodations before capacity is assessed. Record the accommodation and whether it changed comprehension or response ability.

Separate cognitive/behavioral interpretation from dysarthria, respiratory fatigue, hand weakness, gait impairment, ocular-motor limitations, and device-access constraints in ALS and ALS-FTD.

Verify LAR authority under the law applicable to the participant/site; collect participant assent where possible; document dissent; and define a re-consent schedule because capacity can fluctuate or decline.

Treat caregiver/study-partner data as data about a separate person. Avoid allowing the participant or representative to authorize capture of the study partner's voice, face, contact details, or personal observations without that person's permission.
